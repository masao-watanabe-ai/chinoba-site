Research / Public AI & Algorithmic Governance

Public AI Is a Governance System

Public AI is not only a model deployed in government. It is a governance system for making consequential decisions accountable.

When AI shapes who gets support, who is investigated, and who waits, the question is not only how well it predicts. It is who decides, on what authority, with what evidence, and how a citizen can understand, contest, and correct the result. This page treats public AI as an operating design: law, data, models, people, audit, and recourse working as one system.

The 30-Second Version

Three Distinctions That Decide Whether Public AI Can Be Trusted

01

Prediction ≠ Decision

A score or recommendation is an input. An administrative decision applies rules and judgment to a person’s case, and someone must own it.

02

Automation ≠ Accountability

Automating a step does not move responsibility to the software or the vendor. The institution must still be able to say who decided, and why.

03

Transparency ≠ Recourse

Publishing how a system works does not help a resident who was wrongly refused. They need reasons they can act on, and a route to correction.

01 / Why Public AI Is Different

Accuracy Does Not Justify a Public Decision

Public AI appears in government procedures, healthcare policy, education, transportation, disaster response, and welfare. In each, the output can change what a person receives, owes, or is able to access.

What makes the public setting different

  • Rights and access are at stake. Decisions touch benefits, burdens, opportunities, and trust.
  • Errors spread. A mistake harms the individual, then the credibility of the institution.
  • Residents often cannot opt out. They cannot take their case to another government.
  • Accuracy is necessary, not sufficient. A decision also needs a legal basis, reasons, and a path to challenge.
  • The agency stays answerable. Buying a system does not transfer accountability to the vendor.

Efficiency is not the same as public value

A system can process more cases and still lower public value if people unfamiliar with digital tools can no longer reach a service. A medical AI is not usable with confidence if patients cannot understand its basis. An education AI can widen inequality if access differs by family circumstances.

Public AI therefore has to ask who benefits and who may be left behind, not only how many cases were closed.

Commercial AI vs Public AI

DimensionTypical commercial AIPublic AI
ObjectiveRevenue, productivity, customer lifetime valuePublic benefit, fairness, safety, inclusion, social trust
Who is affectedCustomers who can usually switch providerResidents who often have no alternative
Cost of errorA lost sale, a refund, a churned accountA lost benefit, an unjust burden, damaged trust in the institution
What justifies a resultPerformance against a business metricPerformance, plus legal basis, reasons, and a way to contest
Who answers for itThe product team or vendorThe public institution, even when a vendor built the system

The comparison is about emphasis. Commercial systems also have duties to customers; the difference is that public decisions carry legal authority over people who cannot easily walk away.

02 / Core Principle

Separate Prediction, Decision, and Authority

“The AI recommended it” and “the agency decided it” are different statements. Public AI governance begins by keeping them apart.

Five things that must not be merged

LayerQuestion it answersOwnerWhat goes wrong when merged
AI prediction or recommendationWhat does the data suggest about this case?System owner, with the vendor’s documentationA score is treated as a finding of fact
Administrative judgmentGiven the rules and this person’s circumstances, what is the right outcome?Case officerJudgment shrinks to clicking “accept”
Legal or policy authorityWho is empowered to decide, under which rules, within which limits?Legislature, agency head, policy ownerA model quietly redefines eligibility
Human approval and accountabilityWho signs this decision and answers for it?Named decision owner“The system decided” leaves no owner
Citizen notice and recourseCan the person understand, contest, and obtain correction?Appeals function independent of the recommendationAppeal re-runs the same score

If one actor or one system fills several rows at once, the later rows stop being independent checks.

Signal is not decision

AI is good at generating signals: a risk score, a trend, a match. What matters socially is which signals are adopted, under which boundaries, and under whose responsibility. Decision Trace Model describes that path:

Event ↓ Signal ↓ Context ↓ Decision ↓ Boundary ↓ Human Gate ↓ Execution ↓ Trace

Even a perfect observation of what people want is not a decision

Proposals such as Yusuke Narita’s 22nd-Century Democracy imagine algorithms inferring what people want from behavioral data. Whatever one thinks of that, it exposes the harder question: “what people want” and “what society can sustainably and lawfully execute” are not always the same.

Law, minority protection, safety, and exceptions still have to be applied by someone. Observation does not replace that step.

Working rule

AI can support a decision. It must not restrict a person’s rights through a classification that no one can explain.

03 / Public AI Governance Architecture

Seven Layers, One Accountable System

Technology, law, operations, and explanation are designed together. Weakness in any layer shows up as a failure for the citizen.

What each layer must settle

LayerWhat it must settleAccountable role
Policy and legal basisPurpose, eligibility rules, who may decide, which rights cannot be overridden, prohibited usesPolicy owner and legal counsel
Data and evidenceProvenance, quality, minimization, consent, and the line between facts and inferencesData steward and data protection officer
Model / algorithmWhat it recommends, which factors it uses, known limits, and its tested error profileSystem owner
Human decision-makingWho decides, with what evidence and authority to override, and how reasons are recordedNamed decision owner
Audit and monitoringDecision trace, fairness and drift measures, incident thresholds, and the power to suspendInternal audit and independent evaluator
Citizen explanation and appealNotice content, deadlines, an independent review route, and what remedy is availableAppeals function
Vendor and procurement boundaryContracted logs, documentation, audit access, change notice, subcontractors, and exitProcurement officer and system owner

The vendor boundary wraps data and model, not the decision. The agency can buy components; it cannot buy the decision-making responsibility.

These are design patterns for discussion, not legal advice. Which controls are mandatory, and how strictly, depends on the jurisdiction and on how consequential the decision is.

Controls only work if they feed back

Decision logs make audit possible, audit makes improvement possible, and re-evaluation closes the loop.

05 / Lifecycle

From Policy Intent to Recourse, and Back to Policy

Monitoring, appeal, suspension, and redesign are not the end of a project. They are part of operating the system.

The question at each stage

StageQuestion to answerRecord produced
1 · Policy intentWhat public purpose is this serving, and is AI the right tool?Purpose statement, legal basis
2 · Eligibility and decision rulesWhich rules are fixed by law, and which leave room for judgment?Rule specification, authority map
3 · Data preparationWhere does the data come from, and does it encode past inequity?Provenance and quality notes
4 · Model development or procurementWhat may the system recommend, and what does the contract guarantee?Specification, contract terms
5 · Impact assessmentWho could be harmed, and how would we find out?Fairness, privacy, and rights assessment
6 · Controlled rolloutCan it start small, with limits and a way back?Pilot scope, stop conditions
7 · Human reviewDoes the reviewer have evidence, time, and power to disagree?Review and override records
8 · Citizen noticeDoes the person know what happened, why, and what they can do?Notice and delivery proof
9 · Appeal and remedyIs review independent, timely, and able to change the outcome?Appeal file and outcome
10 · Monitoring and auditAre outcomes, disparities, and drift staying within limits?Monitoring reports, audit findings
11 · Policy revisionWhat should change in the rules, data, model, or contract?Revision decision and rationale

A system without stages 9 to 11 is a launch, not a lifecycle.

06 / Decision, Explanation, and Recourse

What Happens When a Citizen Is Told No

The path to remedy has to exist before the first adverse decision, and it has to work for someone with no technical background.

A usable notice says

  • what was decided, and by whom or which office
  • that an AI system supported the assessment
  • the main facts and the rule that led to the outcome
  • how to correct wrong data and by when
  • how to appeal, and who reviews it

Recourse has to be real

  • The reviewer can see the underlying evidence, not only the score.
  • The reviewer is not the person or system that made the first recommendation.
  • Inferences such as “this household needs support” are labelled as inferences and can be corrected.
  • Appeal outcomes are counted, and a high reversal rate triggers review of the system.
07 / Failure Modes and Safeguards

Eight Ways Public AI Fails, and What Prevents Each

Most failures are not exotic. They are predictable consequences of missing design or missing operation.

FailureHow it arisesDesign safeguardOperating safeguard
Historical bias becomes automated exclusionTraining on past decisions that reflected unequal access or enforcementQuestion whether the label (for example, past “detected fraud”) measures the policy outcomeDisparity monitoring by group; approval before retraining
Proxy variables conceal discriminationPostcode, language, school, or employment gaps stand in for protected traitsFeature review and an allow-list of permitted inputsTest outcomes across groups, not only inputs
Automation bias weakens reviewStaff defer to a confident score, especially under workload pressureShow evidence before the score; require reasons when agreeing with high-impact recommendationsTrack agreement and override rates; sample audits; training
Opaque vendor systems prevent accountabilityProprietary models, no log access, undisclosed updatesContract for logs, documentation, and audit rightsChange notification and independent testing
Model drift changes outcomes silentlyPopulation, policy, or data sources change; the model is updatedVersion pinning and release approvalDrift thresholds and re-assessment before updates go live
Citizens cannot contest a decisionNotice says “the system decided” with no reasons or routeContestability built into the notice and processAppeal time targets; independent reviewers; outcomes feed audit
A technically correct model violates policy intentIt optimizes a proxy such as throughput or predicted fraud rather than the policy goalTie metrics to policy outcomes, such as successful access to support and missed-support ratePeriodic policy review of what the system actually does
“Human-in-the-loop” becomes a rubber stampThe reviewer lacks authority, time, or informationAuthority to override; escalation path; reasons recordedMeasure review time, override rate, and reversal on appeal

A near-zero override rate is not proof the system is right. It may mean no one is really reviewing.

08 / Practical Public-Sector Scenarios

Where the Line Falls in Six Common Uses

Generalized patterns for discussion. Where each line falls in practice depends on the law, the risks, and the evidence gathered locally.

i

Welfare and benefit prioritization

Ordering outreach or case backlogs so people likely to miss support are found sooner.

AI supportsFlags incomplete applications and likely missed entitlements.

Humans decideEligibility, amount, and any denial. Lower priority never removes the right to be assessed.

Evidence and recordFactors used, rule cited, caseworker.

Explanation and remedyNotice with reasons; appeal to a unit independent of the ranking.

ii

Fraud and risk detection

Surfacing anomalies in claims or payments for human inquiry.

AI supportsFlags patterns and explains which signals triggered the flag.

Humans decideWhether to open an inquiry. No suspension or penalty rests on a flag alone.

Evidence and recordFlag reasons, supporting evidence, investigator, false-positive rate.

Explanation and remedyThe person can respond before adverse action; wrongful flags are corrected and counted.

iii

Housing, education, and employment support

Identifying who may benefit from limited programs.

AI supportsProposes candidates; labels each “needs support” as an inference, not a fact.

Humans decideSelection criteria are set by policy; staff confirm each placement.

Evidence and recordCriteria, inference labels, who confirmed.

Explanation and remedyPeople, guardians, or teachers can correct data and request reconsideration. Learners are not given fixed scores.

iv

Inquiry and application support

Helping residents find the right program and complete the right steps in order.

AI supportsAnswers questions, checks missing documents, and sequences related procedures such as those after a birth or job loss.

Humans decideLegal judgments. Uncertain or rights-affecting cases return to a responsible professional through a Human Gate.

Evidence and recordPolicy documents cited, their version, and the handoff.

Explanation and remedyA clear route to a person and to written confirmation of what applies.

v

Urban operations, transport, and disaster

Forecasting and resource allocation across agencies.

AI supportsPredicts floods, estimates damage, proposes routes, shelters, and who may need assistance.

Humans decideResponsible personnel confirm important decisions. A person’s wishes and legal rights are not overridden because a plan looks optimal.

Evidence and recordForecast version, assumptions, approver, timeline.

Explanation and remedyAfter-action review and a channel to report people missed or wrongly allocated; coverage checked for older adults and people with disabilities.

vi

Audit and administrative investigation

Reviewing large document sets for inconsistencies or risk.

AI supportsSummarizes documents, links related records, and highlights inconsistencies.

Humans decideFindings, conclusions, and sanctions rest on verified evidence, not model output.

Evidence and recordSource documents, tool version, reviewer verification.

Explanation and remedyThe subject can see the evidence relied on and respond to it.

09 / Beyond Single Procedures

Public Services Are Fragmented. A Life Is Not.

Residents live one life across government, healthcare, education, and welfare. Public AI can help connect support across those boundaries without erasing them.

From transactions to continuing support

Services are usually organized as applications: a resident applies, the agency reviews, a result is sent. But a birth, a job loss, or a diagnosis changes a whole situation at once, and the person is left to find each program and repeat the same information.

If an AI can understand a person’s current circumstances, the programs available, and their deadlines, it can guide them through one coherent journey. This is a navigation role. It does not mean the AI decides eligibility.

Connect, do not merge

The aim is not one giant database. Each domain keeps its own responsibility and authority, and information moves only under informed consent and clear boundaries, when necessary.

Health and education need tighter boundaries

In healthcare, boundaries have to settle:

  • what information the AI may access
  • what stays limited to diagnostic support
  • which decisions need a physician’s confirmation
  • patient consent, emergency escalation, and retention

Presenting diagnostic candidates is not making a diagnosis, and presenting options is not applying them. The trace should record which symptoms, results, and guidelines were considered, and who confirmed.

In education, inferences must be separated from facts and open to correction by learners, guardians, and teachers. Judging friendships or personalities is highly risky, and AI should support change rather than assign fixed scores.

Measure public value, not only volume

Useful indicators include procedure completion, successful access to support, time from consultation to resolution, missed-support rate, and reduction of the digital divide, rather than cases processed or cost cut alone.
